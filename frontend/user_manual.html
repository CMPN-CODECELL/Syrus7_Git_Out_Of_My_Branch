User Handbook & Reference Guide

This manual covers all terminology, valuation formulas, Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) options models, Greek sensitivities, and technical analysis indicators used across the Shani Option Chain Analytics platform.

1. Option Chain & Market Terminology

Core Market Parameters

  • Spot Price (S): Live cash market price of underlying stock or index (e.g., NIFTY 50).
  • Strike Price (K): Specified price at which an option contract can be exercised.
  • Expiry Date: Official settlement date on which option contracts expire.
  • Call Option (CE): Financial contract granting buyer right (not obligation) to BUY underlying asset at strike price.
  • Put Option (PE): Financial contract granting buyer right (not obligation) to SELL underlying asset at strike price.
  • Last Traded Price (LTP): Most recent market execution price of option contract.

Moneyness & Order Book Terms

  • In-The-Money (ITM): Contract with intrinsic value. Calls with strike < Spot, Puts with strike > Spot. Highlighted in pale yellow in option chain.
  • At-The-Money (ATM): Strike price closest to current underlying spot price.
  • Out-of-The-Money (OTM): Contract with zero intrinsic value (time value only). Calls with strike > Spot, Puts with strike < Spot.
  • Bid Price / Qty: Highest price buyer willing to pay and quantity requested.
  • Ask Price / Qty: Lowest price seller willing to accept and quantity offered.
  • Volume: Total number of option contracts traded during current session.
  • Implied Volatility (IV): Annualized market volatility implied by current market price of option contract.

2. Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) Pricing Engine

BSM Model Control Parameters

BSM model calculates theoretical fair price for European options using key parameters:

  • Volatility (σ %): Annualized standard deviation of underlying asset returns.
  • Days to Expiry (T): Remaining time converted to annual fraction T = Days / 365.
  • Risk-Free Rate (r %): Prevailing annual risk-free interest rate (e.g. 6.50%).
  • Dividend Yield (q %): Continuous dividend yield of underlying asset.
BSM Call Formula: C = S · e-qT · N(d₁) - K · e-rT · N(d₂)
BSM Put Formula: P = K · e-rT · N(-d₂) - S · e-qT · N(-d₁)

Theoretical Edge & Pricing Highlights

Comparing market LTP with BSM theoretical price identifies mispriced options:

  • Underpriced (Teal Green): Market LTP < BSM Fair Price. Indicates theoretical underpricing (buying edge).
  • Overpriced (Orange Red): Market LTP > BSM Fair Price. Indicates theoretical overpricing (selling edge).
Theoretical Edge: Edge = LTP - BSM Price. Negative edge indicates underpriced contract; positive edge indicates overpriced contract.

3. Option Greeks & Sensitivity Metrics

Greek Sensitivity Reference Table

Greek Definition & Formula Call Range Put Range Trading Meaning
Delta (Δ) ∂V / ∂S — Sensitivity to 1-unit underlying price change. 0.0 to +1.0 -1.0 to 0.0 Equivalent stock share position & approximate ITM probability.
Gamma (Γ) ∂²V / ∂S² — Rate of change of Delta per price move. > 0 (Peak ATM) > 0 (Peak ATM) Measures Delta acceleration. High near expiry for ATM options.
Vega (ν) ∂V / ∂σ — Price sensitivity to 1% change in volatility. > 0 > 0 Exposure to implied volatility spikes (e.g. before earnings/events).
Theta (θ) ∂V / ∂t — Time decay loss per day. < 0 (Negative) < 0 (Negative) Daily option value erosion rate. Accelerates in final 30 days.
Rho (ρ) ∂V / ∂r — Price sensitivity to 1% interest rate change. > 0 < 0 Impact of interest rate changes (more significant for LEAPs).
BSM Model Intermediaries:
d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r - q + σ²/2)T] / [σ√T]
d₂ = d₁ - σ√T
N(x): Cumulative standard normal distribution function.

4. Technical Indicators & Quantitative Signals

Composite Sentiment Score

The Technical Analysis tab evaluates 7 core technical indicators (RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, SMA 20, SMA 50, EMA 20, EMA 50) and aggregates them into an overall market bias:

  • STRONG BULLISH: ≥ 5 indicators give Bullish signal.
  • BULLISH: Bullish indicators outnumber Bearish.
  • NEUTRAL: Equal Bullish and Bearish count.
  • BEARISH: Bearish indicators outnumber Bullish.
  • STRONG BEARISH: ≥ 5 indicators give Bearish signal.

Oscillators & Momentum

  • RSI (Relative Strength Index - 14): Momentum oscillator measuring speed and magnitude of recent price moves on scale 0 to 100.
    RSI = 100 - [100 / (1 + AvgGain / AvgLoss)]
      • ≥ 70 (Overbought): Bearish alert (asset may be overextended). • ≤ 30 (Oversold): Bullish alert (potential upward reversal). • 55 to 70: Bullish momentum. • 30 to 45: Bearish momentum.
  • MACD (12, 26, 9): Moving Average Convergence Divergence trend-following momentum metric.
    MACD Line = EMA(12) - EMA(26) | Signal = EMA9(MACD)
      • Strong Bullish: MACD > 0 and Histogram > 0. • Strong Bearish: MACD < 0 and Histogram < 0.

Moving Averages (SMA & EMA)

Moving averages smooth price action to identify trend direction:

  • SMA 20 & SMA 50 (Simple Moving Average): Unweighted arithmetic mean of closing prices over 20 or 50 periods.
    SMA = (∑ Prices) / N

    Signal is Bullish when Spot Price ≥ SMA, Bearish when Spot Price < SMA.

  • EMA 20 & EMA 50 (Exponential Moving Average): Weighted moving average applying higher weight to recent prices.
    EMA = Price · k + EMA_prev · (1 - k), k = 2 / (N + 1)

    Signal is Bullish when Spot Price ≥ EMA, Bearish when Spot Price < EMA.

Volatility & Pivot Support/Resistance

  • Bollinger Bands (20, 2): Volatility bands placed 2 standard deviations above and below 20-period SMA.
    Bands = SMA20 ± (2 · σ)

    Spot ≥ Upper Band = Overbought (Bearish alert). Spot ≤ Lower Band = Oversold (Bullish alert).

  • ATR (Average True Range - 14): Non-directional volatility metric calculating average absolute price range per period. High ATR = elevated volatility.
  • Floor Pivot Points: Intraday support and resistance levels computed from prior High (H), Low (L), and Close (C):
    Pivot (P): (H + L + C) / 3
    Resistance R1: 2P - L | R2: P + (H - L) | R3: H + 2(P - L)
    Support S1: 2P - H | S2: P - (H - L) | S3: L - 2(H - P)
